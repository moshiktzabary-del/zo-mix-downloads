Changing the patch

Change the patch, and the gains come back at one press

Every engineer knows this one: change the patch, and each channel takes the gain of its new preamp. Instead of going channel by channel and trying to remember, ZO-Mix gives each channel back the gain and 48V it had.

💾

1. Save

Each time you save the file on the console, ZO-Mix keeps the channels' gain and 48V. You can also press Save gains. Without a save nothing is kept, not even when a file loads.

🔀

2. Change the patch

At the next show, load the file and change the patch on the console, as always.

🎚

3. Recall gains

Hold for 2 seconds, with a progress bar, and every named channel returns to the saved values. A line on screen says how many came back.

🛡

Safe for the system

Gains go down first, then 48V, and only then up. Before 48V goes on, the screen names the channels and asks. Undo brings it all back.

🎯

One channel too

Need just one? Choose it and recall only that, or tick which channels to bring back.

🔎

A unique name on every channel

A channel that moved to another number is found by its name, so each channel needs its own: TOM 1 and TOM 2, not TOM twice. Channels sharing a name go back by number, and channels with the console's default name are left alone.

Snapshots

A snapshot notebook, with a colour for every song

The console's snapshot window: Fire, next, previous, update, insert, rename, renumber, move and delete. Colours show at a glance which snapshots belong to one song. A lock keeps choosing and firing open, and edits open only by a long press.

How it works

Three steps

1. Install on the Mac and enter a name and email. 2. On the console, in External Control, add a device of type DiGiCo with the Mac's address, Send 52300 and Rcv 52301. 3. Musicians scan a QR code, type a name and choose an instrument.

FAQ

Questions

Does ZO-Mix change things on the console?

Yes. Faders, mutes, sends, EQ, snapshots and more are written to the console. That is why there are locks and permissions, and why it is worth trying first at a soundcheck.

What has it been tested on?

Quantum 338, Quantum3 and SD10 on DiGiCo's Offline software. Physical consoles and other models are expected to work but have not been verified, which is why this is a pre-release.

What does pre-release mean?

The software is complete and notarized by Apple, and we are still gathering experience from the field. Your feedback directly shapes the next versions.

Do musicians need to install an app?

No. They scan a QR code or open an address in the browser.

Is internet needed?

Not at the show. It is needed to download, to activate a license and to register; registration is sent later if there is no connection.

Is there timecode or metering?

Not yet. We have found no way to timecode over OSC, and the meters are not calibrated yet.

Does it run on Windows?

Not for now: Macs with Apple Silicon only.
