Changing the patch

Change the patch, and the gains come back with one hold

Every engineer knows this one: change the patch, and each channel takes the gain of its new preamp. Instead of going channel by channel and trying to remember, ZO-Mix gives each channel back the gain and 48V it had.

💾

1. Save

Each time you save the file on the console, ZO-Mix keeps the channels' gain and 48V. You can also press Save gains. Without a save nothing is kept, not even when a file loads.

🔀

2. Change the patch

At the next show, load the file and change the patch on the console, as always.

🎚

3. Recall gains

Hold for 2 seconds, and every named channel returns to the saved values. A line on screen says how many came back.

🛡

Safe for the system

Gains go down first, then 48V, and only then up. Before 48V goes on, the screen names the channels and asks.

🎯

One channel too

Need just one? Choose it and recall only that, or tick which channels to bring back.

🔎

A unique name on every channel

A channel that moved to another number is found by its name, so each channel needs its own: TOM 1 and TOM 2, not TOM twice. Channels sharing a name go back by number, and channels with the console's default name are left alone.

A router for the show

A steady network matters more than speed

ZO-Mix carries control data, not audio, so even a small router can do. At a show, what counts is range and stability.

📍

High, with a line of sight

Not inside a rack or behind the console. A crowd and metal gear weaken the signal.

📡

5GHz, a fixed channel

We suggest channel 36–48 and 40MHz width, not Auto, so the network does not jump during a show.

📶

Measure on site

With the band on stage, at the farthest musician. Aim for -67 dBm or better. If that falls short, move to a professional access point.

FAQ

Questions

Can a musician mess up the PA?

A musician changes only the sends of their own mix, which does not touch the PA. You choose which mixes are open, and you can lock everything.

What happens to chat messages?

They stay until the app closes, and they do not pass through the internet or the console.

What if a musician loses Wi-Fi?

They cannot send a message, but the engineer sees that they dropped off. When the network is back they connect by themselves, with no new approval.

Does ZO-Mix change things on the console?

Yes. Faders, mutes, sends, EQ, snapshots and more are written to the console. That is why there are locks and permissions, and why it is worth trying first at a soundcheck.

What has it been tested on?

A physical Quantum 225, and Quantum 338, Quantum3 and SD10 on DiGiCo's Offline software. Other models are expected to work but have not been verified, which is why this is a pre-release.

What does pre-release mean?

The software is complete and notarized by Apple, and we are still gathering experience from the field. Your feedback directly shapes the next versions.

Do musicians need to install an app?

No. They scan a QR code or open an address in the browser.

Is internet needed?

Not at the show. It is needed to download, to activate a license and to register; registration is sent later if there is no connection.

Is there metering or timecode?

There are pre-fader meters on every input, and they show signal with the mute closed. The scale is still relative, with no dB numbers or clip light. We have found no way to timecode over OSC.

Can I see Mustard processing?

Not yet. In the OSC tree we checked on a Quantum 225, we found no Mustard addresses.

What if a device loses the network during a show?

It reconnects by itself when the network returns, with the same permissions and no further approval.

Does it run on Windows?

Not for now: Macs with Apple Silicon only.
